Passive Sonar Quick Reference

Passive sonar is a listening system that detects and analyzes sounds emitted by targets without transmitting any signals. It is the primary detection method for submarines operating in stealth mode.

How Passive Sonar Works

Basic Principle

Passive sonar systems use hydrophones (underwater microphones) to detect acoustic energy radiated by targets. The system analyzes frequency, bearing, and amplitude characteristics to identify and track contacts without revealing the listener's position.

Key Components

1. Hydrophones & Arrays

Arrays of hydrophones arranged along the hull, towed behind the vessel, or deployed on the seabed. Multiple hydrophones enable bearing determination through phase comparison.

Diagram: Hydrophone Array Configuration
(Hull-mounted, towed, and flank arrays)

2. Signal Processing

Advanced algorithms filter noise, perform frequency analysis (FFT), and extract target signatures from background ocean noise. Narrowband and broadband processing techniques identify different sound sources.

(Broadband vs. narrowband tonals)

3. Display Systems

Acoustic data presented on waterfalls, BTR (Bearing-Time-Recorder), and LOFAR (Low Frequency Analysis and Recording) displays for operator analysis.

Passive vs. Active Sonar

Passive Sonar

  • Stealth: Does not reveal position
  • Range: Can detect targets at very long ranges
  • Limitation: Requires target to make noise
  • Data: Bearing only (no direct range)
  • Use: Continuous monitoring, classification

Active Sonar

  • Detection: Can find quiet targets
  • Range: Provides accurate range data
  • Limitation: Reveals own position
  • Data: Bearing and range
  • Use: Attack, navigation, short-range detection

Sound Sources Detected

Target Radiated Noise

  • Machinery: Pumps, turbines, generators, reduction gears
  • Propulsion: Propeller cavitation, blade rate tonals
  • Hydrodynamic: Flow noise, hull vibrations
  • Transients: Hatches, torpedoes, dropped objects
Diagram: Submarine Noise Sources
(Cross-section showing machinery, propeller, and flow noise)

Key Operational Points

  • Environmental factors affect detection: temperature layers, salinity, biologics
  • Target quieting technology continuously improves, requiring better sensors
  • Classification relies on acoustic signature databases and operator experience
  • Convergence zones can provide detection at extended ranges in deep water
  • Own-ship noise must be minimized for effective passive detection
